Context

As the second-largest financial company in Russia, Sber provides competitive, secure, and profitable career opportunities for more than 40,000 IT employees. However, to convert qualified candidates into invaluable team members, the company must communicate its distinct brand and value while offering tangible benefits. This was our main challenge when I joined the Technobrand team in mid-2023. At that time, the website – intended to attract candidates – was technically functional but offered a broken user experience. Over 18 months, we transformed a messy platform into Sber IT's primary talent gateway.

Research & Discovery

Before designing solutions, I needed to understand where Technobrand fit in the candidate's broader job search ecosystem. For this reason we contacted an in-house UX Researcher to work on the Research Methodology that would suit the needs of the platform. 

After several meetings discussing research objectives, project constraints and business goals we defined several research goals:

Which part our website takes in the candidate's employment journey? 

What are the expectations vs. reality gaps in the candidate journey?

Who is our portal actually serving — and who should it serve?

Based on the research goals we came up with a research methodology to gather valuable insights to redesign the website:

Interview Recent Hires – qualitative interviews of developers hired within 6 months to generate hypotheses from people who successfully navigated the hiring process

Build User Personas – 2-3 candidate personas with distinct needs and behaviors to define who the tech portal should serve

Usability Testing – task-based usability testing on current portal to validate hypotheses with candidates actively job searching

Quantitative Validation – statistical validation of problem severity to validate qualitative findings at scale

Ideation + Concept Development – design workshops with stakeholders to generate solutions to validated problems

Concept Testing – preference testing / prototype testing to validate proposed solutions before development

After conducting 5 qualitative interviews with recent Sber applicants we collected the whole CJM of how a candidate becomes an IT Sber Employee. 

The "Entrance Gate" Insight

Based on the CJM and further conversations with stakeholders, I discovered a critical strategic insight: Technobrand isn't the entire job search journey – it's an "entrance gate" moment. We exist at the critical juncture between interest and action.

Critical Problems Discovered

Based on gathered User Personas we highlighted general problems our website had:

Trust & Credibility

Users who found the site didn't trust it or understand its purpose. Minimal design and generic visuals reinforced skepticism.

Vacancy Page Difficult to Use

Users couldn't find relevant opportunities among 100+ mixed vacancies. No location, work mode, or experience filtering existed.

Vague and Unclear Benefits

Benefits were presented vaguely with generic micro-copy and stock graphics, making it difficult for candidates to evaluate opportunities.

Cannot Scale

No design system meant developers struggled to implement each new component. External Sber divisions featured on Technobrand appeared inconsistently.

Design Decisions

Lead with Technology Impact

Instead of generic corporate messaging, I opened with Sber IT's most recognizable offerings: cutting-edge technology (Supercomputer Christofari, Kandinsky AI, GigaChat) and the bold statement "We inspire ourselves with the best, we conquer heights."

Why: Competitive research of Yandex, T Bank, and Avito career sites showed that IT professionals want to know what they'll build, not just what they'll get. This also addressed the CJM insight: "Want strong team and non-trivial tasks."

Thematic Organization

I grouped benefits into 5 scannable categories users care about:

🏢 Office – Best Office Award winner, Sber City access, 90 days work-from-anywhere

📚 Learning – Virtual school for Mid-level developers, Corporate University for Seniors, IT Bootcamp for new employees

💚 Care – Competitive salary, health insurance from day 1, 4% below-market loan rates

💼 Work – Hybrid schedule, full remote options, offices across Russian cities

✨ Additional – Free SberPrime+ subscription, partner discounts, industry recognition

Clear categorization lets users quickly scan to their priorities rather than reading everything linearly. This addresses: "I needed to understand whether I'm a fit" – users can self-assess across multiple dimensions.

Visual Credibility Through Authenticity

I replaced random stock photos with branded, authentic Sber imagery: real offices, actual product screenshots (Kandinsky, GigaChat generating images), and specific numbers rather than vague promises (not "competitive rates" but "4% below-market loan rates").

The inconsistent visuals in the old version created trust issues, echoing the CJM finding: "No trust in site. Nothing indicated official Sber channel." Using real Sber offices, actual products, and consistent branding clearly communicates that this is an official page.

Dual-Purpose Design: Online + Offline

I designed the page to work for both online discovery (accessible from main menu) and offline recruitment (accessed via QR code at conferences and career fairs).




Why: At an IT conference, HR used QR codes directing attendees to this page. The consolidated benefits showcase allowed candidates to review everything on their phones, share with colleagues, and apply directly. Result: ~100 applications generated from that single event in October 2024.

Iterating Based on Usage

After launch, we tracked metrics of how frequently each menu section is visited and noticed many users skipped the page after landing on Technobrand. Most online users followed this path: Main page → Careers → Vacancy page → Apply.

To confirm our observations, we conducted additional UX testing and asked: "What do you need to know before applying for a position?" Most participants mentioned: "I prefer to find most things on the vacancy page itself. I want it to be comprehensive and detailed so I can save it for later."

Action taken: We enriched each vacancy page with benefits we'd gathered before. Thanks to our block design approach, we were able to extract pieces from the full page and insert them into vacancy pages separately. The "Learn more" link connects these embedded benefits with the comprehensive "Why Us" page.

Design Process

As part of my design process, I gathered Jobs-to-be-Done from our CJM and performed competitive research to investigate relevant patterns and UX best practices. I developed several drafts based on this research, and with my manager, we prioritized decisions.

Design Solutions

Comprehensive Filter System

What we implemented:

Quick access filters by project field 

Non-scrollable left sidebar filters – Location checkboxes, work mode (Hybrid/Office/Remote), experience levels

Active filter display – Shows applied filters with ✕ removal buttons

Real-time vacancy count – Immediate feedback (e.g., "100 vacancies")

Non-scrollable sidebar – Always accessible while browsing

This directly solved the CJM complaint about missing remote filters. Stakeholders identified "too many irrelevant results" as a key issue. Multi-dimensional filtering (category + location + work mode + experience) lets users narrow 100+ vacancies to 10-15 relevant matches quickly. Left sidebar filters are a proven pattern from competitive research – familiar and scannable for users.

Systematized Vacancy Cards

The old cards had random colors, minimal information, and no personality. I redesigned them with:

Recruiter prominence – Photo, name, team (supports CJM preference for direct contact)

Individual team logos/branding – SberData, Platform V for differentiation

Consistent tag system – Blue pills for location/work mode, neutral tags for city and experience

Clear visual hierarchy – Better spacing and scannability

Old cards were "not engaging enough." Systematized colors create scannable patterns – users can quickly evaluate multiple opportunities. Recruiter photos and team branding add human connection and personality, addressing emotional engagement gaps.

Modern Visual Design

I added a bold visual banner with 3D graphics and "Realize your potential in IT" messaging, matching the benefits page aesthetic.

Visual research of Yandex, Tinkoff, and Google career sites showed that polished design signals legitimacy. The hero banner establishes credibility and emotional engagement before users dive into filtering.

The banner is also designed in Sber's branded blue color, strengthening visual consistency between pages.

Solution 3: 
Design System & External Guidelines

Goals Addressed: ⚡ Reduce Lead Time | 🎨 Establish Brand | 🏢 Scale Entrance Gates | 🤝 Enable Teams

As Technobrand grew, we needed to onboard more Sber divisions onto the platform. This required us to communicate our brand and design standards clearly and precisely, enabling teams to create content independently without bottlenecking our small team.

Without standardized guidelines, each new division required full designer involvement. We couldn't scale to support all the Sber teams wanting to be featured on Technobrand.

My Solution

I created a UI Kit with two core components:

Internal component library – Reusable UI elements and guides with detailed specs for developers and external teams 

Reusable templates – Comprehensive template pages enabling other Sber divisions to implement content independently

Part 1: Internal Component Library

I created a UI Kit with reusable components and detailed specifications for our development team. This enabled developers to implement designs consistently without constant clarification questions.

Part 2: Reusable Templates

For external Sber divisions, I developed comprehensive content guidelines. When teams wanted to feature their content on Technobrand, they could follow specific guides for each content type—ensuring brand consistency while working independently.

So I created an IT Hub Page template with scalable blocks that teams could easily populate with their content. For each block, I documented examples and constraints in Confluence, providing clear guardrails for independent content creation.

As a result, teams are enabled to create fill out pages with their content without bothering us.

Key Learnings

What Worked

Strategic framing shaped everything. Understanding Technobrand as an "entrance gate" rather than the full job search journey helped me focus on the right problems: credibility, clarity, and friction-free exploration at the critical moment between interest and action. This insight guided every design decision.

Constraints I Worked Within

Limited measurement capabilities. Without feature-specific analytics, I couldn't prove which design elements drove engagement or optimize based on data. I relied on competitive research, stakeholder feedback, and platform-level metrics to make decisions. While the overall growth suggests success, I can't confidently attribute it to specific design decisions. This taught me to advocate more strongly for analytics and measurement infrastructure.

What I'd Do Differently

1. Advocate for Better Measurement Infrastructure. While we had basic analytics, we didn't have feature-level tracking or A/B testing capability. I should have pushed harder for lightweight instrumentation (event tracking, Hotjar, etc.) to enable data-informed iteration.

2. Conduct More Lightweight Testing Throughout. I did research at the beginning, but should have done quick guerrilla tests before major launches. 15-minute hallway tests or 3-5 user interviews could have caught assumptions early.

3. Evangelize the Design System More Proactively. Only one division adopted the external guide during my tenure. I should have proactively reached out to teams rather than waiting for them to discover it.

Biggest Takeaway

Bringing UX to an organization without that culture requires equal parts design skill and change management. Success wasn't just about making better designs—it was about establishing processes, building relationships with stakeholders and external teams, demonstrating value incrementally, and creating systems that empower others to maintain quality without constant designer presence.
